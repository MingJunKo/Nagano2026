Gravel, packed dirt and boardwalk, almost no climbing. Expect icy patches in the morning in shaded sections, then mud and wet leaves as it thaws. Peaks may be snow-dusted and light valley snow is possible. Autumn colour is mostly past peak, with some larch gold. Check the forecast the night before and shorten the loop if it is wet or snowy.

  • Fleece mid layer
  • Light down or insulated jacket for breaks

  • Warm hat, gloves, neck gaiter
  • Hiking boots or trail shoes with real grip, wool socks

Carry

  • Packed lunch, snacks, 1 to 1.5 L water, flask of something hot
  • Light microspikes for icy mornings (optional)
  • Spare gloves and a spare warm layer
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen (snow glare at 1,500 m)
  • Headlamp as backup, power bank (cold drains phones)

  • Cash: ¥100 coins and ¥1,000 notes for the bus, toilets and Myojin Pond
  • Offline map downloaded. Signal is patchy in the valley

Bears

Bears are active until they den in December and Kamikochi has sightings in autumn. Myojin to Tokusawa and Shinmura is the quietest, most remote stretch. Stay together, talk or use a bell, no headphones, and read the notices at the Bus Terminal before you start.

  • Season and conditions: kamikochi.org. The season ends 15 Nov, so 12 Nov is open.

Running tips for this trip

  • Cold starts. Mountain mornings are around freezing. Wear tights, a long sleeve, a light shell, gloves and a beanie, and peel off after 10 minutes.
  • Frost. Bridges, boardwalks and shaded lanes ice over first. Shorten your stride and slow down on them.
  • Bears. Karuizawa and Hakuba both have bears active until December. Run after sunrise, stay on roads, wear a bell, and skip the headphones.
  • Traffic. Japan drives on the left. Mountain lanes have no sidewalk in places, so run facing the traffic and in single file.
  • Ryokan timing. Breakfast seatings are fixed. Tell the front desk your time the night before, and run before it.
  • Onsen after. Rinse off fully at the wash stations before you get in.

Plan B: Route 292 closed on Day 3

The leg from Kusatsu to Jigokudani crosses Shibu Pass (2,172 m) on Route 292. It closes for winter between mid and late November, and the stretch past Mt Shirane also closes when the volcano warning level rises.

IfThen
292 openDrive it. It's the best scenery of the trip.
292 closed, clear weatherManza Highway detour (toll, ~¥3,000–4,000, +30–45 min). You keep the monkeys.
292 closed, snow or iceLowland route: Route 145 to Ueda, Nagano city (Zenkoji), Hakuba. Open all year, but you drop Jigokudani.

Check the Shiga Kogen road status a week out and again that morning. Ask the Futabaya front desk, or call the Gunma Nakanojo road office on +81 279-75-3047. Winter tyres are not optional. The rest of the route (146, 148/158, Abo Tunnel) is low and open all year.

Driving

  • Drive on the left. Watch for frost in shade and on bridges, especially before 10:00.
  • Legs: Karuizawa → Kusatsu (~45 min) → Jigokudani and Zenkoji → Hakuba → Hirayu (~2 hr) → Matsumoto (~1 hr).
  • Fill up in town before every mountain leg, and return the car full at Matsumoto.
  • No car on Day 6. Private cars are banned in Kamikochi, so leave it at Miyama Ouan.
